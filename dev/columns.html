Columns and flags#

Note

The examples on this page read the UATAQ archive, which is only available on CHPC. They are shown as code and are not run when the documentation is built. The output under each one is from runs on CHPC on 12 October 2026. If the data are reprocessed, the numbers can change a little.

The columns you get depend on the instrument and on the data level you read (raw, qaqc, calibrated or final; see Your first month of data for what each level is for). This page says what the columns are, level by level, for the instruments you are most likely to use. Each section says which instrument and level its examples come from.

The examples print wide tables. These two pandas settings print them in full, whatever the width of your terminal:

>>> import pandas as pd
>>> import uataq
>>> pd.set_option("display.width", 120)
>>> pd.set_option("display.max_columns", None)

In every table#

  • The index is Time_UTC, the time of the measurement in UTC, without a time zone attached. Your first month of data shows how to convert it to local time.

  • The concentration columns follow one naming pattern for every instrument, level and research group: CO2d_ppm_cal, O3_ppb, PM2.5_ugm3 and so on. The Pollutants page explains the pattern of the names (the d means a dry mole fraction, the last part is the unit).

  • uataq renames many of the instruments’ other columns to common names, so that a quantity has the same name across instruments and groups. For example, the lgr_ugga’s cavity pressure is Cavity_P_torr in the pipeline’s files and Internal_P_torr in uataq. The renaming is in lin.column_mapping and horel.column_mapping. Keep this in mind when you read the pipeline’s own documentation, which uses the names in its files.

The CO2 and CH4 analyzers#

This section is about the lin group’s greenhouse gas analyzers: the lgr_ugga (a Los Gatos Research analyzer, which measures CO2 and CH4) and the licor_6262 and licor_7000 (LI-COR analyzers, which measure CO2 only). They are the only instruments with a calibrated level. Each row is one record of the analyzer, about every 10 seconds. The examples read the lgr_ugga at WBB on 2 June 2025 and the licor_7000 at SUG on the same day.

Level

What it has

final

The concentrations and H2O_ppm, for the outside-air rows that passed QAQC. No ID or flag columns.

calibrated

Every row, with the calibrated and uncalibrated concentrations, the calibration fit, the ID_CO2 (and ID_CH4) columns and QAQC_Flag.

qaqc

Every row, with all of the analyzer’s columns, ID, ID_CO2 (and ID_CH4), and QAQC_Flag. Not calibrated.

raw

The analyzer’s columns as they were logged, with ID but without ID_CO2, ID_CH4 or QAQC_Flag. Reading one day of the WBB lgr_ugga at this level took 159 seconds, against 1 or 2 seconds at the others.

Column

Meaning

CO2d_ppm_cal

CO2 dry mole fraction in ppm, calibrated against the site’s reference tanks. Can be empty; see When _cal is empty.

CO2d_ppm_raw

The same measurement before calibration.

CH4d_ppm_cal, CH4d_ppm_raw

The same for CH4, in ppm (lgr_ugga only).

H2O_ppm

Water vapor in ppm, taken from the qaqc level.

The licor_7000 has the same columns without the CH4 ones:

>>> uataq.read_data("SUG", "licor_7000", time_range="2025-06-02")["licor_7000"].columns.tolist()
['CO2d_ppm_cal', 'CO2d_ppm_raw', 'H2O_ppm']

get_obs() returns the concentration columns of this level, so get_obs("WBB", "CO2") gives CO2d_ppm_cal and CO2d_ppm_raw.

The pipeline calibrates each analyzer with the reference tanks it measures every few hours: it fits a straight line between the tanks’ measured and known values, for every row. The pipeline’s calibration page describes the method. The fit columns, for CO2 (the CH4 ones start with CH4d_):

Column

Meaning

CO2d_ppm_raw

The uncalibrated value: the qaqc level’s CO2d_ppm. Empty on flagged rows (all 8,362 flush rows at WBB in June 2025), except rows flagged for an out-of-range reading, which keep it.

CO2d_m, CO2d_b

The slope and intercept of the fit. The calibrated value is CO2d_ppm_cal = (CO2d_ppm_raw - CO2d_b) / CO2d_m.

CO2d_n

How many reference tanks the fit used at that time. With one tank the fit is a slope only (CO2d_b is 0). With none, there is no fit and CO2d_ppm_cal is empty.

CO2d_rsq, CO2d_rmse

The fit’s R² and root mean square error (in ppm). Empty when the fit used fewer than three tanks.

ID_CO2, ID_CH4

What the analyzer was sampling; see What the analyzer was sampling.

QAQC_Flag

The qaqc level’s flag, unless the calibration flagged the row itself (see Flags).

The formula holds to rounding:

>>> ((cal["CO2d_ppm_raw"] - cal["CO2d_b"]) / cal["CO2d_m"] - cal["CO2d_ppm_cal"]).abs().max()
np.float64(2.8421709430404007e-12)

QAQC#

>>> qaqc = uataq.read_data("WBB", "lgr_ugga", lvl="qaqc", time_range="2025-06-02")["lgr_ugga"]
>>> qaqc.columns.tolist()
['CH4_ppm', 'CH4_ppm_sd', 'H2O_ppm', 'H2O_ppm_sd', 'CO2_ppm', 'CO2_ppm_sd', 'CH4d_ppm', 'CH4d_ppm_sd', 'CO2d_ppm', 'CO2d_ppm_sd', 'Internal_P_torr', 'Internal_P_torr_sd', 'Internal_T_C', 'Internal_T_C_sd', 'Ambient_T_C', 'Ambient_T_C_sd', 'RingDown0_us', 'RingDown0_us_sd', 'RingDown1_us', 'RingDown1_us_sd', 'Fit_Flag', 'ID', 'ID_CO2', 'ID_CH4', 'QAQC_Flag']

Column

Meaning

CO2_ppm, CH4_ppm, H2O_ppm

The mole fractions in the sampled (wet) air, in ppm, not calibrated.

CO2d_ppm, CH4d_ppm

The dry mole fractions, not calibrated. They are the _raw values of the calibrated and final levels.

*_sd

A standard deviation that the lgr_ugga’s software writes beside each value ([CO2]d_ppm_sd in the analyzer’s own files). The LI-COR analyzers have none.

Fit_Flag

A status number that the lgr_ugga’s software writes with each row. uataq and the pipeline pass it through unchanged, and the pipeline’s QAQC does not use it. Every row of this day has 3.

ID, ID_CO2, ID_CH4

What the analyzer was sampling; see the next section.

QAQC_Flag

The pipeline’s flag; see Flags.

Internal_*, Ambient_T_C, RingDown*

The analyzer’s own measurements of its state (cavity pressure and temperature, and so on), with their _sd.

The licor_6262 and licor_7000 have other instrument columns at this level (flow, pressure, temperature, the logger’s battery voltage). Print columns to see them.

What the analyzer was sampling#

The analyzers do not only sample the outside air. A valve switches them to the reference tanks for a few minutes every few hours, and the rows logged while the lines are flushed after a switch are marked as well. Three columns say which is which.

ID is the label logged with each row. For the lgr_ugga it is text, the description set on the analyzer’s Multi Inlet Unit (the valve box). ID_CO2 and ID_CH4 are numbers that the pipeline makes from it:

>>> qaqc.groupby(["ID", "ID_CO2", "ID_CH4"]).size()
ID                      ID_CO2   ID_CH4
~406.49~1.91             406.49   1.91       70
~478.24~2.62             478.24   2.62       63
~520.00~5.63             520.00   5.63       63
~Atmosphere~Atmosphere  -10.00   -10.00    8390
~flush~flush            -99.00   -99.00     259
dtype: int64

ID_CO2 / ID_CH4

Meaning

-10

The outside air (ID is ~Atmosphere~Atmosphere).

-99

A flush (ID is ~flush~flush).

a positive number

A reference tank. The number is the tank’s known CO2 (or CH4) in ppm, which ID holds as ~CO2~CH4.

So for the lgr_ugga, select the outside air with ID_CO2 == -10. ID == -10 selects no rows, because ID is text.

For the licor_6262 and licor_7000, ID is already a number, with the same codes, and ID_CO2 is equal to it. They have no ID_CH4:

>>> licor = uataq.read_data("SUG", "licor_7000", lvl="qaqc", time_range="2025-06-02")["licor_7000"]
>>> licor["ID"].value_counts()
ID
-10.00     7992
-99.00      459
 398.22      60
 453.73      60
 487.55      60
 524.29       5
Name: count, dtype: int64
>>> (licor["ID"] == licor["ID_CO2"]).all()
np.True_

The tank rows pass QAQC. The flush rows are flagged (-2), but the reference tank rows have flag 0, like the outside air:

>>> pd.crosstab(qaqc["ID_CO2"], qaqc["QAQC_Flag"])
QAQC_Flag   -2     0
ID_CO2
-99.00     259     0
-10.00       0  8390
 406.49      0    70
 478.24      0    63
 520.00      0    63

So QAQC_Flag >= 0 alone keeps the tank rows. The final level is the calibrated rows of the outside air that pass QAQC:

>>> kept = cal[(cal["ID_CO2"] == -10) & (cal["QAQC_Flag"] >= 0)]
>>> len(kept), len(final)
(8390, 8390)
>>> kept.index.equals(final.index)
True

The same holds for the licor_7000 at SUG that day (7,992 rows). The final level follows the calibrated level’s flags, which can differ from the qaqc level’s because the calibration adds flags of its own. For the licor_6262 at SUG in June 2019, the calibration flagged 566 rows -4; the rule above gives the 238,632 final rows from the calibrated level, but 239,149 from the qaqc level.

When _cal is empty#

Some rows of the final level have a _raw value and an empty _cal. There are two reasons.

The instrument has no calibrated level. The pipeline then fills only the _raw columns. This is the case for TRX01’s lgr_ugga_manual_cal (see Pollutants for what its _raw holds):

>>> trax = uataq.read_data("TRX01", "lgr_ugga_manual_cal", time_range="2024-09-01")["lgr_ugga_manual_cal"]
>>> trax.notna().sum()
CO2d_ppm_cal       0
CO2d_ppm_raw    7200
CH4d_ppm_cal       0
CH4d_ppm_raw    7200
H2O_ppm         7200
dtype: int64

There was no calibration fit at that time (CO2d_n is 0). These rows are not flagged, so they are in the final level. The pipeline calibrates each calendar year on its own, so the hours of a new year before the first tank measurement have no fit. At WBB on 1 January 2025:

>>> jan1 = uataq.read_data("WBB", "lgr_ugga", time_range="2025-01-01")["lgr_ugga"]
>>> empty = jan1["CO2d_ppm_cal"].isna() & jan1["CO2d_ppm_raw"].notna()
>>> int(empty.sum()), jan1.index[empty].max()
(824, Timestamp('2025-01-01 02:14:01'))

The calibrated level shows the fit starting as the analyzer measures the three tanks, one after another. Here is the first row with each value of CO2d_n:

>>> cal = uataq.read_data("WBB", "lgr_ugga", lvl="calibrated", time_range="2025-01-01T02")["lgr_ugga"]
>>> cols = ["CO2d_ppm_raw", "CO2d_ppm_cal", "CO2d_n", "CO2d_b", "CO2d_rsq", "ID_CO2"]
>>> cal.drop_duplicates("CO2d_n")[cols]
                     CO2d_ppm_raw  CO2d_ppm_cal  CO2d_n    CO2d_b  CO2d_rsq  ID_CO2
Time_UTC
2025-01-01 02:00:00      445.0774           NaN       0       NaN       NaN  -10.00
2025-01-01 02:15:48      406.0314    406.425037       1  0.000000       NaN  406.49
2025-01-01 02:18:44      477.9684    478.134889       2 -1.685318       NaN  478.24
2025-01-01 02:21:40      519.6304    519.871474       3 -0.750496  0.999997  520.00

The same happens after the last tank measurement of a year (at WBB from 23:14 on 31 December 2024). Reading all of 2025 at WBB and CSP also found such rows in the first two to five hours of several months (at WBB from September, at CSP from July). In every case seen, CO2 and CH4 were empty on the same rows. The licor_7000 at SUG had none in 2025.

To find these rows in your data:

empty = df["CO2d_ppm_cal"].isna() & df["CO2d_ppm_raw"].notna()

Their _raw value is not calibrated.

Other instruments#

The lin group’s other instruments (the Teledyne analyzers, the 2B ozone monitor, the MetOne particle monitor, and so on) have no calibrated level and no _cal or _raw columns. Their qaqc level has the instrument’s columns and QAQC_Flag; their final level keeps the rows that pass QAQC and only a few columns. For the teledyne_t400 (ozone) at WBB on 2 June 2025:

>>> ozone = uataq.read_data("WBB", "teledyne_t400", lvl="qaqc", time_range="2025-06-02")["teledyne_t400"]
>>> ozone.columns.tolist()
['O3_std_ppb', 'O3_Meas_mV', 'O3_Ref_mV', 'O3_Gen_mV', 'O3_Drive_mV', 'Photo_Power_mV', 'Internal_P_inHgA', 'Flow_mLpm', 'Internal_T_C', 'Photo_Lamp_T_C', 'O3_Scrub_T_C', 'O3_Gen_T_C', 'Instrument_T_C', 'Slope', 'Offset', 'O3_ppb', 'Test_mV', 'QAQC_Flag']
>>> uataq.read_data("WBB", "teledyne_t400", time_range="2025-06-02")["teledyne_t400"].columns.tolist()
['O3_ppb']

The pipeline lists the columns of every instrument and level in its data_config.json, with the names in its files, before uataq renames them.

The horel group’s instruments#

The TRAX trains and the buses also carry instruments run by the horel group (ozone, particles, NOx; see Research Group on the general functions page). Their qaqc and final levels are read from the same horel files. For the ozone (2b_205) and particle (metone_es642, metone_es405) monitors, the horel files mark a flagged row with 1, and uataq turns that into QAQC_Flag = -1, so the flag is 0 (passes) or -1 (flagged). The final level is the qaqc rows that pass and have a value. For the 2b_205 on TRX01 on 1 June 2024:

>>> o3 = uataq.read_data("TRX01", "2b_205", lvl="qaqc", group="horel", time_range="2024-06-01")["2b_205"]
>>> o3.columns.tolist()
['O3_ppb', 'Flow_Lpm', 'Internal_T_C', 'Internal_P_hPa', 'QAQC_Flag']
>>> o3["QAQC_Flag"].value_counts()
QAQC_Flag
 0    39134
-1     4066
Name: count, dtype: int64
>>> final_o3 = uataq.read_data("TRX01", "2b_205", group="horel", time_range="2024-06-01")["2b_205"]
>>> kept = o3[(o3["QAQC_Flag"] >= 0) & o3["O3_ppb"].notna()]
>>> kept.index.equals(final_o3.index)
True

The -1 of a horel row means only that the horel group flagged it. The flag numbers on the pipeline’s page are the lin group’s.

Flags#

QAQC_Flag is in the qaqc and calibrated levels of the lin group’s instruments, and in the qaqc level of the horel group’s ozone and particle monitors. Zero and positive flags pass; a negative flag fails, and its number says why. The final level has no flag column: it holds only rows that passed.

What each number means is on the pipeline’s QAQC flag page, grouped by instrument, with the calibration’s own flags under Calibration. It names the columns as they are in the pipeline’s files (see In every table): its Flow_CCmin, for example, is uataq’s Flow_mLpm, or Flow_Lpm for the 2b_205.
